# Supplementary Material S14: Extended Baseline Characteristics and Neuropsychological Profile

## Overview

The main text Table 1 summarizes core demographics for the DDM analytic sample. This supplement provides (1) an **extended baseline characteristics table** including the full neuropsychological battery and (2) **exploratory associations** between cognitive/affective measures and DDM parameters.

**Data:** `data/raw/LC Aging Subject Data master spreadsheet - demographics.csv` and `... neuropsych.csv`  
**Script:** `scripts/neuropsych_demographics_supplement.R`

## Extended baseline characteristics (N = 67 DDM sample)

The analytic sample comprised older adults (*M*~age~ = 69.5 years, *SD* = 6.4; 34 female, 51%) with high educational attainment (*M* = 15.8 years, *SD* = 2.6). All participants were right-handed.

Telephone MoCA scores (*M* = 19.8, *SD* = 1.5; *n* = 62) were above the study inclusion threshold (< 17), confirming generally intact global cognition. Affective symptoms were low (GDS *M* = 1.7; GAD-7 *M* = 2.3). The sample was 50.7% female, 100% right-handed, 73.1% White, and 15.4% Hispanic/Latino ethnicity.

See @tbl-extended-baseline in Appendix A9 for full descriptives.

## Exploratory neuropsych ↔ DDM associations

Partial correlations controlling for age and sex did **not** identify reliable associations between global cognition (MoCA) and baseline drift (*v*; *p* = .94), accuracy effort cost (*p* = .16), or RT effort cost (*p* = .66). Thus individual differences in the primary effort → drift effect cannot be attributed to gross cognitive impairment in this sample.

Exploratory associations surviving *p* < .05 (none FDR-corrected at *q* < .05) included:

| Predictor | Outcome | Partial *r* | *p* |
|---|---|---|---|
| SILS | Accuracy | .37 | .003 |
| SILS | Mean RT | −.36 | .004 |
| Education | Accuracy | .30 | .015 |
| GDS | d′ variability | −.27 | .034 |
| GDS | Baseline bias (*z*) | −.25 | .049 |

These are interpreted cautiously as secondary individual-differences context — language/semantic ability (SILS) correlated with overall task accuracy and RT, and lower depressive symptoms trended with more stable perceptual sensitivity — but they do not modify primary DDM inferences.

## Relation to LC supplement (S13 / Appendix A8)

Both supplements characterize **who** the participants are and **what individual differences co-vary** with task performance, without changing group-level DDM conclusions. The neuropsych profile confirms a cognitively intact, highly educated sample appropriate for mechanistic DDM decomposition.
